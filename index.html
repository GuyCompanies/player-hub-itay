What the trend says

Top speed is a strength, not a development area. Across 10 sessions his max speed ranged 21.6–30.8 km/h, peaking at 30.8 km/h on 18 Aug and touching 30.6 km/h in the 07 Aug match — two sessions at or above 30 km/h, plus five more in the 27–29 km/h band. The training-only trend across the 9 non-match sessions is upward (+6% early-to-late split, slope +0.18 km/h/session), so his ceiling is climbing, not just a one-off.

The engine (work-rate) is climbing. Total distance and m/min both trend up across training sessions (distance +6%, m/min +6% comparing his first 3 to his last 3 training sessions), with personal bests on 11 Aug (7,485 m, 82.8 m/min) and 12 Aug (7,116 m, 75.3 m/min) — both far above his own average and the squad average. He ranked #1 in the squad for total distance and m/min in 4 of the last 5 training sessions.

Sprint output is trending sharply up. Distance covered at 25+ km/h rose from single digits in early August to 88 m (12 Aug) and 195 m (18 Aug) — a +445% early-to-late increase — consistent with the rising top-speed ceiling.

  • Outlier to flag: 10 Aug was his weakest session across the board (21.6 km/h max, lowest of the whole squad that day, and his personal low for distance/accel/decel) — worth checking with the coach whether this was a recovery/rotation day, minor knock, or a genuinely off session.
  • 07 Aug match: very low total distance (2,768 m) but very high intensity-per-minute (106.2 m/min, 30.6 km/h peak) — reads as a short, high-impact substitute cameo rather than a full 90.
  • Deceleration load is consistently well above team average (latest 32 vs team 18.2, +76%) — combined with his high sprint volume, this is a normal signature of an explosive wide player, but worth monitoring for injury-prevention conditioning as training load builds.
